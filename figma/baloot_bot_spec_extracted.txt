Baloot AI Bot SystemComplete 3-Tier Implementation Spec for CursorEasy  ·  Medium  ·  HardPURPOSE: This document tells Cursor exactly how to build three AI bot difficulty levels for the Baloot card game. Each level simulates a different human experience level: Easy = 6-month beginner who just learned, Medium = 1-year regular player, Hard = 5+ year expert player. The bots must FEEL like real humans, not like obvious computers.Section 0 — Overview of all 3 difficulty levelsKey design rule: bots should NEVER be labelled "BOT" to other players. Give them Arabic names (e.g. خالد، سعد، نورة، ريم). Players should not know with certainty whether they are playing a human or a bot. This is the most important single rule in this entire document.FeatureEasyMediumHardWhy it mattersExperience level6-month beginner1-year regular5+ year expertSets the entire personality of the botThinking delay3–5 seconds (random)1.5–3 sec + occasional 5s "thinking"0.8–2 sec + rare 6s "hesitation"Realistic timing prevents instant-move detectionMistake rate35% suboptimal moves15% suboptimal moves5% intentional soft mistakeHumans always make mistakes — bots must tooTrump card memory0% — forgets immediately60% of played trumps95% of ALL played cardsMemory is the clearest skill gap between levelsPartner coordinationNone — plays aloneBasic — avoids overtrumping partnerAdvanced — reads partner signalsReal Baloot is a team game — bots must reflect thisBidding strategyRandom within legal rangeCounts high cards, bids if 3+ trump cardsFull hand evaluation + reads opponent styleBidding is where player skill is most visibleExpressionsOccasional random onlyContextually appropriate, ~40% of the timeContextually perfect, timed naturallyMakes the bot feel alive and emotionalWhere bots are usedTutorial, practice mode onlyFill disconnected player slots mid-gameFill matchmaking slots at peak timesDifferent use cases require different skill ceilingsSection 1 — Easy Bot ("مبتدئ" — 6-month beginner)This bot should feel like someone who just learned Baloot 6 months ago. They understand the basic rules but make obvious strategic mistakes. They are predictable. They are NOT stupid — they never break the legal rules of the game. They just make bad strategic choices. Use this bot ONLY for the tutorial and practice mode.1.1  Thinking delay — how long before the bot plays a cardAlways wait between 3,000ms and 5,000ms before playing any cardPick a random value in this range: delay = Random(3000, 5000)Never instant-play (0ms). Never too slow (>6000ms)Variation is what makes it feel human — never use a fixed 3000ms every timeFor bidding decisions: delay = Random(2000, 4000)For the last card of a trick: delay = Random(1500, 3000)  (slightly faster — less to think about)1.2  Bidding behaviourBid threshold: ONLY bid Hokum if the bot has 3 or more cards of the face-up suit in handLess than 3 of that suit: always Pass3+ of that suit: 70% chance bid, 30% chance Pass even then (indecisiveness)Sun (no-trump): NEVER bids Sun in round 1 — too advanced a call for a beginnerAshkal: NEVER bids Ashkal — too advancedSecond round (ثاني): if forced to bid in round 2, 50% Pass (ولا), 50% Hokum on any suit with 2+ cardsDouble/Triple/Four: NEVER doubles, triples, or fours — does not understand risk escalationGahwa (counter after double): NEVER uses gahwa1.3  Card playing behaviour — the 35% mistake systemThe Easy bot plays a LEGAL card every time — it never cheats or breaks rules. But 35% of the time it plays the worst legal option instead of the best. This simulates a beginner who knows the rules but not the strategy.On each turn, generate a random number 0–100:0–34 (35% chance): play the WORST legal card (highest value card that cannot win the trick)35–100 (65% chance): play the best available legal cardTrump card usage: throws trump even when partner is winning the trick (classic beginner mistake)Jack of trump (highest trump): plays it too early — in the first 3 tricks if given the choiceSuit following: always follows suit correctly (legal rule — never breaks this)When leading a trick (goes first): plays a random card from hand — no strategy in which suit to leadSaving high cards: NO concept of saving Aces for critical moments — plays them whenever legally possible1.4  Project declarations (سرا، خمسين، مية، بلوت)Always declares all projects it has — no hiding, no tactical withholdingDeclares in order of highest value first (not strategic, just eager to show off)Baloot (King + Queen of trump): always declares it as soon as the second card is played — no delayDoes NOT check if declaring is tactically smart — just declares everything1.5  Memory and trackingPlayed card memory: 0% — the bot has NO memory of which cards have been playedTrump count: does not track how many trumps remain in playOpponent hand inference: does not infer anything about opponent or partner handsResult: the bot will sometimes play a card that a real player would know is useless (e.g. low card on a suit where all higher cards are still out)1.6  Expressions and social behaviourUses expressions randomly — not contextually. 20% chance to send ANY expression on any turnNever calibrates expression to game state — might send "celebrate" when losingBot "name" examples: سعد، طارق، خالد، ماجد  (never label as "BOT")Avatar: basic default avatar, low-level rank badge (Beginner rank)1.7  Specific edge cases for Easy botMaqfal (لعب محدود — restricted mode): never locks, never uses restriction strategicallyKawesh / Saneen: NEVER declares kawesh even if it has a valid kawesh handCutting the deck (Free play mode): cuts randomly 50% of the time, never withholdsAwal / Thani bids: participates randomly, no strategic reasoningSection 2 — Medium Bot ("متوسط" — 1-year regular player)This bot must feel like a real player who has been playing Baloot for about a year. They understand strategy, make smart bids, remember which trumps have been played, and coordinate with their partner. They make occasional mistakes but mostly play solid Baloot. This bot is used to replace DISCONNECTED players mid-game.2.1  Thinking delay — variable, contextualStandard turn: Random(1500, 3000)ms — faster than Easy, feels more confident"Thinking" turns (15% of decisions): Random(4000, 6000)ms — simulate a hard decisionTriggered when: holding multiple viable options with similar valueTriggered when: the round score is close (within 20 points of target)Triggered when: a project was just declared by an opponentBidding delay: Random(1500, 2500)ms + 500ms extra if holding a borderline handAfter partner plays a surprising card: add 800ms extra delay (reacting to unexpected play)2.2  Bidding behaviour — strategicCounts trump suit cards in hand + high cards (Jack, 9, Ace, 10) to decide:4+ cards of face-up suit OR 3 cards including Jack: always bid Hokum2 strong cards (Jack + Ace or Jack + 9): 70% bid, 30% pass2 weak cards only: 20% bid, 80% pass1 or 0 cards: always passSun bid: bids Sun if hand has 4+ Aces/Tens across all suits and no strong single suitAshkal: uses Ashkal correctly — only in 3rd/4th position, only if partner would benefit from the cardSecond round (ثاني) strategy:Looks for best suit in hand (most cards + highest value cards)Bids Hokum Thani on that suit if it has 3+ cards of any suitBids Sun Thani if no suit has 3+ cards but has multiple high cards across suitsSays Wala (ولا) only if hand is genuinely weak across all suitsDouble: doubles if own team won last 2 rounds consecutively AND has strong remaining cards (40% chance)Gahwa: uses gahwa if team was doubled AND has high confidence in hand (50% chance)2.3  Card playing — the 15% mistake systemMedium bot plays the BEST legal card 85% of the time. 15% of the time it plays a suboptimal (but not worst) card. This simulates occasional lapses in concentration that real 1-year players have.On each turn, generate a random 0–100:0–14 (15%): play the SECOND-best legal card instead of best (soft mistake, not catastrophic)15–100 (85%): play the mathematically best legal cardTrump management: ONLY plays trump when necessary:When opponent is winning a trick and bot cannot follow suitWhen holding Jack of trump and knows opponents have no higher trump (from memory)NEVER overtrumps when partner is already winning the trickSuit leading (going first in a trick):First 3 tricks: lead with Ace if available — clear the fieldMid-game: lead with suit partner is likely strong in (inferred from bids)Late game: lead with trump if bot is winning — force opponents to spend their trumpsAce/Ten preservation: saves Aces and Tens for tricks where they are likely to winDump strategy: when cannot win a trick, plays lowest-value card to minimise point loss2.4  Card memory — 60% trump trackingTracks which trump cards have been played with 60% accuracy:60% chance of correctly remembering a trump played 2+ tricks ago40% chance of "forgetting" — bot acts as if it never saw that cardNon-trump cards: only remembers cards played in the CURRENT trick (short-term only)Uses memory for: deciding whether to play Jack (if 60%+ confident all higher trumps gone)Suit voidness tracking: remembers with 70% accuracy when opponent showed void in a suite.g. if opponent played trump instead of following clubs → bot notes "opponent may be void in clubs"2.5  Partner coordination — basic teamworkDoes NOT overtrump when partner is winning a trick — always checks firstDumps high-value cards (Aces, Tens) to PARTNER when partner is winning a trickSpecifically throws Aces and Tens to partner in the last 2 tricks to maximise team pointsSuit signalling (basic): if bot leads with Ace of non-trump suit, this signals strength to partnerReads partner bid: if partner bid Hokum on Hearts, bot assumes partner has strong Heart hand and leads HeartsNO advanced communication — no suit-throw signals beyond leading with an Ace2.6  Project declarations — tacticalCalculates whether declaring a project is beneficial before declaringIf opponent has already declared a higher project → bot withholds lower projectsOnly declares if it helps the team (adds more points than it reveals to opponents)Baloot declaration: waits until strategically good moment — not immediately on first qualifying cardHides سرا (20-pt) if opponents have higher projects — saves the reveal for when it matters2.7  Expressions and social behaviourContextually appropriate 40% of the time:After winning Kaboot: sends celebrate expressionAfter losing a critical trick: might send "oops" or disappointed expressionAfter opponent declares 400: sends surprised expression60% of the time: no expression (realistic — most real players don't spam expressions)Bot name examples: عبدالله، فيصل، نورة، سارة  (regional Saudi names, realistic)Rank badge: Good–Advanced tier (♦♦♦ to ♠♠♠♠)2.8  Personality archetype (pick one randomly per game session)PersonalityBehaviourTell-tale signالحذر (Cautious)Passes more, only bids when very confident. The Hard bot must feel like playing against a real 5-year veteran of Baloot. It plays near-perfectly but NOT perfectly — a 5-year player still makes the occasional mistake, gets unlucky, and shows personality. If it plays perfectly 100% of the time, experienced players will immediately detect it as a bot. The 5% intentional imperfection is critical.3.1  Thinking delay — natural expert timingStandard: Random(800, 2000)ms — experts play faster, they know what they're doing"Fake hesitation" (15% of turns): Random(4000, 7000)ms — simulating a genuinely hard decisionDoes NOT always mean the bot is actually uncertain — often the outcome is clear, but bot hesitates to seem humanTriggered deliberately to break the pattern — even on obvious moves sometimesFast plays (20% of turns): Random(500, 900)ms — expert snapping the card down quicklyTriggered when: leading a trick with a card they planned 2 tricks agoTriggered when: dumping a low card on a partner's winning trickBidding: Random(1000, 2000)ms — experts know their hand fastException: borderline hand (bid or not?) → always 3,000–5,000ms to simulate real deliberation3.2  Bidding — full expert evaluationFull hand scoring before every bid decision:Jack of face-up suit = 4 pointsNine of face-up suit = 3 pointsAce of any suit = 2 pointsTen of any suit = 1 pointEvery additional card of face-up suit beyond first = 1 pointBid thresholds:Score 6+: confident Hokum bidScore 4–5: Hokum bid with 75% probability, Pass 25%Score 3: Pass in round 1, consider in round 2Score <3: Pass (ولا)Sun evaluation: if Aces + Tens ≥ 5 and no suit has Jack + 3 more cards → strong Sun bidAshkal: uses Ashkal when partner's seat gives them the face-up card and partner bid aggressively earlierDouble/Triple/Four: doubles if team has won 3+ of last 4 tricks. Triples if 4-of-4. Four almost never (90% confidence only)Gahwa after double: bot fires Gahwa if hand score was 6+ when originally bidAwal (claim priority): uses Awal if in 1st or 2nd position with a hand score of 7+3.3  Card playing — near-perfect with intentional imperfectionThe hard bot uses full card tracking and optimal play. BUT: 5% of the time it plays a deliberately SOFT mistake — not a game-losing blunder, just a slightly suboptimal choice. This one imperfection per game on average is what makes it undetectable as a bot.On each turn, generate 0–100:0–4 (5%): intentional soft mistake — play the second-best card (never worst)5–100 (95%): full optimal card selection algorithm (see 3.4)Trump management — expert level:NEVER plays trump when partner is winning the trickSaves Jack of trump until opponents have played at least 2 other trumps OR until last 2 tricksLeads with trump to drain opponents only when holding 2+ trumps and opponent has not shown voidCounts exact remaining trump before playing Jack — only plays if confident no higher trump remainsLeading strategy:Tricks 1-2: clear Aces from non-trump suits to bank points earlyTricks 3-4: lead with suit where partner is strongest (inferred from bid and previous plays)Tricks 5-6: switch to pressure — lead with suits opponents might be void inTricks 7-8: use trump strategically to win the last high-value tricksAce/Ten timing: NEVER plays Ace/Ten into a trick the team will lose. Saves them for partner-winning tricks.Maqfal (locking): actively uses locking restriction in Free Play mode to prevent opponents from leading strong suits3.4  Full card memory — 95% accuracyRemembers 95% of ALL played cards (not just trump) throughout the entire gameImplements a "seen cards" set. 5% chance of "forgetting" any individual card seen 5+ tricks agoUses memory for:Exact trump count remaining — knows when it is safe to play JackSuit void inference — if player X did not follow clubs twice → X is void in clubs with 90% confidenceHand reconstruction — after 5 tricks, can estimate with 80% accuracy what each opponent holdsPoint calculation — running total of points in current round, adjusts strategy if behindUses point calculation to shift strategy mid-round:If team is 20+ points behind: switches to aggressive mode — takes risks to win key tricksIf team is 20+ points ahead: switches to safe mode — dumps low cards, lets partner win3.5  Advanced partner coordinationReads partner bid thoroughly:Partner bid Hokum on Spades → bot infers partner has 3+ Spades including probably JackPartner bid Sun → infers partner has strong Aces/Tens across suits, no single dominant suitPartner passed in round 1 but bid in round 2 → infers moderate hand, not exceptionalSuit signalling:Leads with specific suit to signal strength to partner (leading with 10 of suit = strong signal)Deliberately dumps 10 to partner in their winning trick = "I have nothing left in this suit, you control it"Partner protection: if partner is at risk of Kaboot (all 8 tricks going to opponents) → bot sacrifices optimal play to win ONE trick and prevent KabootKaboot hunting: if opponents are at 7 tricks (1 away from Kaboot) → bot plays trump even suboptimally to stop it3.6  Project strategy — tactical withholdingNEVER automatically declares all projects:Calculates whether declaring helps or hurts the team's total positionIf opponent has 100-pt project → bot only declares its own 100-pt if it ties or beatsDeliberately withholds سرا (20-pt) if it gives opponents information they'd exploitBaloot (King + Queen of trump): declares only after Jack is played or won — maximises tactical value of the reveal400-point project: always declares immediately — too valuable to withhold, and it's demoralising to opponents3.7  Personality archetypes — 5 options for hard bot (one per game session)PersonalityCore traitBidding behaviourCard play behaviourالقاهر (Crusher)Extremely aggressive — hunts for Kaboot every Pick one at random per game session. Same name stays for the whole game.Assign a random rank badge appropriate to bot level:Easy bot: Beginner or Amateur rank badgeMedium bot: Good or Advanced rank badgeHard bot: Expert or Professional rank badgeUse a random pre-made avatar from your avatar library (not the default grey silhouette)4.2  Legal rules — bots MUST follow these perfectly 100% of the timeBots making illegal moves (playing wrong suit when holding the correct suit, bidding out of turn, etc.) immediately destroys the game experience. The mistake system above only ever plays a LEGAL but suboptimal card — never an illegal one.Always follow suit if the bot holds a card of the led suitMust play a trump if cannot follow suit AND trump is being played for (لعب محدود rules)Cannot play a higher trump than necessary (overtrump rule in some Baloot variants — implement per your game rules)Must declare projects at the correct moment (first trick only)Cannot bid out of turnCannot play a card that is not in their hand4.3  Disconnection replacement behaviourWhen a human player disconnects and a bot replaces them:Easy mode game: replace with Easy botRanked game: replace with Medium bot (slightly below average — fair replacement)The bot takes over MID-TRICK if necessary — picks up from exact game stateBot receives the disconnected player's current hand exactly as-isDo NOT inform other players that a bot replaced the human — maintain the illusionIf human reconnects mid-game: immediately restore human control, release bot4.4  Economy impact — bot gamesBlue stars earned from bot games: capped at maximum 40 stars per bot game (no bonuses)Medals earned from bot games: capped at maximum 10 medals per bot gameThis applies to all 3 bot levels — the cap is the same regardless of difficultyWinning against the Hard bot still gives capped points — to prevent "farming" the botShow a small indicator after the game if points were capped: "Playing against humans gives more points"Section 5 — Implementation guide for Cursor5.1  Data structures// Bot configuration object class BotPlayer {   final BotDifficulty difficulty;
